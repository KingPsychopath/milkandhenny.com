# Ticket transfer and refund policy

This is the product-policy baseline for event configuration and customer copy. It is an
engineering record, not legal advice; obtain UK legal review before relying on it for a material
event or changing the product into a resale service.

## Product boundary

Milk & Henny supports assignment and gifting of an existing ticket. It does not provide ticket
listings, resale pricing, buyer/seller messaging, escrow, private-payment tracking, commission,
transfer fees, or buyer protection for a private payment. A transfer changes the attendee who may
use one ticket; it does not change the purchaser, the order, or the original payment method.

Customer-facing terms should say, in clear language:

- Transfers are ticket reassignment or gifting, not a Milk & Henny resale marketplace.
- Milk & Henny does not arrange or protect private payments between attendees.
- Refunds return only to the original payment method.
- The event's entry restrictions, ticket type, date, location information, and face price shown at
  checkout remain part of the ticket terms.
- A transferred ticket cannot be refunded silently. The purchaser and current holder must both
  consent, unless the event is cancelled or another stated legal right applies.
- A ticket that has checked in cannot be transferred or automatically refunded; support reviews
  the door record.

## Operational rules

- Refund one ticket allocation at a time and identify the amount and affected ticket before
  confirmation.
- Cancel a pending transfer before starting its refund.
- Rotate ticket-link and QR authority when a transfer is accepted.
- Keep transfer availability globally off and event-level off until explicitly enabled.
- Treat complimentary tickets as returns/cancellations with no payment refund.
- For event cancellation, refund the original payer and notify every current holder.
- Keep financial, consent, and authority audit records separate from public attendee identity.

## Review record

Reviewed on 25 August 2026 against the official [Consumer Rights Act 2015, Part 3 Chapter
5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/pdfs/ukpga_20150015_en.pdf) and the Department for
Business and Trade [secondary-ticketing guidance for
business](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/secondary-ticketing-websites-guidance-for-business).
Those sources regulate internet-based facilities for ticket resale and require specified ticket
information to be clear before a resale contract. Keeping this product out of listing, sale, and
private-payment intermediation is therefore an intentional product boundary, not a substitute for
legal review. General consumer-protection and primary-ticket obligations still apply.
